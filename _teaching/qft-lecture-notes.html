QFT is an application of QM to dynamical systems of fields, very much like QM concerns the quantization of dynamical systems of particles.

\[\underset{\text{non-rel}}{\text{QM}} \oplus \underset{v\to c}{\text{SR}} \;\Rightarrow\; \text{rel. QM} \checkmark \;\to\; \text{QFT (so far)}\]

"QM": particle–wave duality (\(e,\ \gamma\)) \(\Rightarrow\) Particles vs. Field.

Classical viewpoint

  • Particles are fundamental objects. \(e \Rightarrow\) elementary object of Nature.
  • Fields are derived, e.g. light \(\leftarrow\) EM field.

Difficulty: Action at a distance (Coulomb's, Newton's law). Interactions are instantaneous — "No locality". \(\Rightarrow\) Introduce SR (\(v\le c\)), fields.

Quantum viewpoint

Due to duality, particle & wave \(\Rightarrow\) similar. View fields as fundamental.

E.g. (photon fields, electron fields, proton fields) — "ripples" \(\Rightarrow\) particles: electron, photon, proton.

  • locality
  • can treat states with unspecified # of particles.

9/6 QFT — Special Relativity

Two principles of special relativity

  1. Relativity: In all coordinate systems related to each other by "uniform velocity", the laws of physics should be the same (frame independence).
  2. The speed of light \(c\) is the same in all frames.

→ Einstein rederived the Lorentz transf. solely based on the above two principles.

Noether's theorem: statement about the dynamical consequence of symmetry, e.g. conservation.

Consider a change of coordinates corresponding to a Lie group with parameters \(\{\alpha_i\}\):

\[x'^\mu=f^\mu(x,\alpha),\qquad \phi'_a(x')=F_a(\phi(x),\alpha)\]
Under this transformation:
\[S=\int_\Omega d^4x\,\mathcal L(\phi(x),\partial_\mu\phi(x))=\int_{f(\Omega)}d^4x'\,\mathcal L'\big(\phi'(x'),\partial'_\mu\phi'(x')\big)\quad(\text{just renaming with prime})\]

\[\int_\Omega d^4x\,\mathcal L(\phi(x),\partial_\mu\phi(x))=\int_{f(\Omega,\alpha)}d^4x'\,\mathcal L\big(\phi'(x'),\partial'\phi'(x')\big)\]
